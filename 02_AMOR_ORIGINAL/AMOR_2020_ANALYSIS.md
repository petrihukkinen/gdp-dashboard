# AMOR 2020 — Reconstruction and Analysis

Source: `01_SOURCE_MATERIAL/AMOR_2020_Executive_Summary_ORIGINAL.pdf` (40 slides). All facts
below are Level A unless marked otherwise; see `EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` for the full citation
table.

## Reconstructed business model

**Structure:** Management buy-out. Four senior Neste MMA (Maintenance & Asset Management)
managers — Hukkinen, Ala-Nikkola, Raassina, Kekkonen — propose forming AMOR Oy, which
acquires the maintenance function for One Refinery (Porvoo + Naantali) from Neste for a
nominal €1, taking on ~350 FTE and continuing the same scope of service MMA already
performed. Spares/materials inventory stays with Neste.

**Earnings model (staged):**
- Years 1–2: open-book cost-plus. AMOR's own cost and administrative cost marked up 25%;
  spares/materials pass through at no markup; Neste provides support functions (CMMS, IT,
  HR, payroll) free of charge during this ramp period.
- Years 3–5: fixed price for a fixed scope, invoiced evenly monthly; anything outside scope
  billed hourly; investment/development projects and turnarounds invoiced separately;
  headroom to sell services to other Kilpilahti-park companies from Year 3, prioritizing
  Neste.

**Value creation levers named:** hands-on-tool-time (reduce waiting time, work-permit
delay), contractor consolidation and integration (AMOR positioned as the single integrator
managing ~600 FTE of ecosystem capacity — Main Service Providers + Other Service Providers —
around a 300-FTE AMOR core), shift from hourly to lump-sum subcontracting, own-organisation
right-sizing (350 → 310 FTE by 2022), and lifecycle/reliability planning for critical and
statutory equipment.

**Governance:** 5-year contract, auto-renewing, earliest exit after year 4 with 12 months'
notice, function reverts to Neste on termination. AMOR proposes its annual budget; Neste
approves it. KPIs are reporting/governance metrics (availability, MTBR, TRIF, PSER, customer
satisfaction, cost), not payment-linked.

**Committed financial case:** €129m 2021 baseline maintenance cost, declining to €112m by
2025 (87% of baseline), a claimed €43m cumulative benefit to Neste over 5 years — against a
"potential" value pool sized at €48–62m **per year** (slide 22), a gap the source document
never reconciles and explicitly caveats as pre-due-diligence (A11).

## Ten strongest principles (worth taking seriously, whether or not they carry forward as-is)

1. Start from a real, itemized cost baseline (€129m broken into 5 categories) — not an
   abstract value story.
2. Name the earnings model explicitly and stage it (open-book → fixed price) rather than
   pretending day-1 pricing certainty is possible.
3. Put the provider's own people's skin in the game — AMOR was led and owned by the people
   who had run the function, not by an outside financial sponsor.
4. Treat contractor consolidation/integration as a distinct, nameable value lever, not a
   footnote.
5. Separate white-collar role redesign from blue-collar/subcontractor productivity — they
   are different problems with different levers.
6. Build in a real contractual exit mechanism (reversion to customer) rather than assuming
   permanence.
7. Explicitly budget for the customer's own transition cost (€150k, A15) — small, credible,
   disarming.
8. Sequence the org build before the pricing model — "organisation first, fixed price
   second" (2021 org work precedes 2023 fixed-price maturity).
9. Name statutory/lifecycle risk (risk-based replacement investment for statutory equipment)
   as an explicit workstream, not an afterthought.
10. Build a real stakeholder/working-group structure (11 named working groups, slide 34) —
    proof the authors had thought through execution, not just economics.

## Ten outdated assumptions (do not carry forward uncritically)

1. Single-customer, single-site model — no evidence AMOR 2020 was designed to work anywhere
   else.
2. KPIs with zero attribution logic — availability and MTBR are named as targets with no
   mechanism to separate maintenance-caused loss from operational/process loss.
3. No gainshare/painshare — the value story is 100% one-directional cost reduction to the
   customer; the provider's only upside is the markup/fixed-fee spread, not shared value
   creation.
4. "Potential" savings (€48-62m/yr) presented alongside a much smaller committed number
   (€43m/5yr) with no reconciliation — a credibility problem baked in from the start.
5. Digital/CMMS treated as a single bullet ("CMMS project started") with no data strategy,
   no AI, no predictive analytics detail.
6. Headcount reduction (350→310) presented as a plan input with no visible workforce
   transition or reskilling narrative beyond "change management promoters."
7. Governance assumes an unusually high-trust, founder-led relationship ("mutual trust and
   respect," slide 26) that may not transfer to an arms-length platform serving many
   customers.
8. No mention of technology/digital moat — the differentiation claimed is entirely
   organisational (leaner org, integrator role), not data or IP-based.
9. Scale plan is one vague bullet — "satellite organisations at nearby industries" (slide
   10) — with no replicability methodology, standard core, or configurable module thinking.
10. Union/labor strategy is named as a lever ("opportunity to rearrange union relations") but
    treated as an execution detail rather than a named strategic risk with its own
    mitigation plan.

## Ten major commercial risks (as they existed in the 2020 proposal)

1. €1 nominal transfer price with no visible valuation methodology disclosed.
2. Unreconciled savings claims (see outdated assumption #4) — the single biggest risk a CFO
   or Red Team would flag first.
3. Full headcount and cost risk transferred to a newly formed, thinly capitalized company
   with no disclosed balance sheet or parent guarantee.
4. Reversion-on-termination clause protects Neste but leaves AMOR's owners exposed if the
   relationship sours after they've built a company around one customer.
5. 25% markup years 1-2 with Neste approving the budget — thin, dependent-party economics
   before AMOR has any independent negotiating leverage.
6. External sales ("other Kilpilahti customers") explicitly subordinated to Neste priority —
   structurally limits diversification exactly when it's most needed (early years).
7. No committed pricing for turnarounds/shutdowns — one of the largest cost and risk items in
   refinery maintenance — left as "invoiced based on separate orders."
8. Union negotiation risk not resourced beyond a single named workstream with no fallback
   plan disclosed.
9. No disclosed HSE/liability allocation for major incidents post-transfer.
10. No stated equity/ownership economics for the four founders beyond "salaried employees" —
    unclear what MBO upside actually existed beyond employment continuity.

## Ten ideas worth carrying into AMOR 2030

1. Real, itemized baseline costing as the starting discipline for any new engagement.
2. Staged commercial maturity (assessment → open-book → fixed price → performance) — this
   maps directly onto the protocol's Phase 0-4 commercial model.
3. Naming an explicit integrator role for a fragmented subcontractor base.
4. Separating white-collar role redesign from blue-collar/subcontractor productivity as
   distinct value pools.
5. Explicit, small, disarming customer transition-cost budgeting.
6. Founder/leadership continuity from the customer's own organisation as a trust-building
   mechanism (translated at scale: staffing key roles with people who understand the
   customer's operations, not generic account managers).
7. A real contractual exit/reversion mechanism, reframed at scale as customer-protective
   contract terms that de-risk the *customer's* decision to buy.
8. Naming statutory/lifecycle risk explicitly as a workstream.
9. A working-group / governance structure for the transition period — reusable as a
   Phase 0/1 mobilization playbook.
10. The instinct to sequence organisational readiness before pricing sophistication — don't
    sell fixed price or gainshare before the delivery organisation can actually perform to
    it.

## The AMOR 2020 → AMOR 2030 logic bridge

| 2020 element | 2030 equivalent | What must change |
|---|---|---|
| Single-site MBO, one customer | Standard core + configurable modules, multi-customer platform | Needs an explicit replicability test (Gate 5) — nothing in 2020 addresses this |
| Cost-plus → fixed price staging | Phase 0-4 commercial staging (assessment → open book → fixed fee → performance fee → gainshare) | 2020 stops at fixed price; 2030 must design the two hardest phases (performance fee, gainshare) that 2020 never attempted |
| Governance KPIs with no attribution | KPI Pyramid + Performance Attribution Matrix | Entirely new work — Gate 2 |
| Integrator managing ~600 FTE ecosystem | Asset Performance Office managing a broader digital + reliability + contractor stack | Add reliability engineering, digital/predictive capability, and value validation governance that 2020 never had |
| Trust-based, founder-led relationship | Joint Asset Performance Board + Value Validation Board | Formalize what 2020 left informal, because it must now work without founder-level personal trust across many customers |
| "Satellite organisations" (one vague bullet) | Explicit Scale Model (Standard Core / Configurable Modules / Customer-Specific) | This is the single largest gap between 2020 and what 2030 needs |
