# Strategic Thesis (Level C — under test)

## The question
Can a large industrial services company create a scalable, higher-value business model by
moving from maintenance delivery towards measurable asset performance outcomes?

## The working thesis
"The next competitive advantage is not cheaper maintenance. It is measurable industrial
performance."

## Working evolution hypothesis
Labour Provider → Maintenance Provider → Integrated Maintenance Partner → Reliability
Partner → Asset Management Partner → Asset Performance Partner → Outcome-Based Strategic
Partner.

## First-pass test against the AMOR 2020 evidence

The 2020 AMOR proposal is the single richest piece of evidence available to this project,
and it cuts against the thesis in an important way: **AMOR 2020 was sold almost entirely on
cost reduction, not performance.** The headline number is €43m cumulative cost savings
(`EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` A6). Of the six named KPIs (A9), five are efficiency/safety metrics
tracked for governance, not paid for; none carry a gainshare or painshare mechanism; and
none separate maintenance-caused performance from customer-controlled or process-driven
performance (`HYPOTHESIS_REGISTER.md` H3). AMOR 2020, in other words, is a highly credible
example of Labour Provider → Maintenance Provider → Integrated Maintenance Partner (the
first three rungs of the ladder), not evidence that the top rungs are commercially viable.

This does not refute the thesis — it means the thesis is untested by the best evidence this
project currently has, and the "hardest" rungs (Asset Performance Partner, Outcome-Based
Strategic Partner) are where AMOR 2020 offers zero precedent. Any claim that AMOR 2030
reaches those rungs needs new evidence, not a repackaging of the 2020 deck.

## Questions this thesis must survive (per protocol; answered where evidence allows, else
flagged UNTESTED)

| Question | Answer after Cycle 1 |
|---|---|
| Is the market actually ready? | UNTESTED. No customer evidence beyond one refinery's 2020 internal deliberations. |
| Do customers want this? Which customers? | UNTESTED. AMOR 2020's customer (Neste/OPP) wanted lower cost and union/organisational flexibility, not performance-based pricing — see `04_CUSTOMER_PROBLEM/`. |
| What will customers refuse to outsource? | Partially answered by AMOR 2020: Neste kept spares/materials ownership (A3), kept approval rights over AMOR's proposed budget (A7/A39 principles), and structured a reversion clause on termination (A4) — i.e. even a friendly, management-led MBO kept meaningful customer control. This is real evidence that full outcome-based control transfer is a hard sell even in a best-case, trust-based scenario. |
| Can performance be objectively attributed? | NOT YET DESIGNED — see `RISK_REGISTER.md`, blocks Gate 2. |
| Can the provider carry the contractual risk? | UNTESTED — no painshare existed in AMOR 2020 to test against. |
| Does the model improve margins / revenue quality? | UNTESTED for the provider side; AMOR 2020's own economics (25% markup years 1-2, fixed price years 3-5) are closer to a managed-services margin profile than a performance-partner margin profile. |
| Is it scalable? | WEAKENED as a default assumption — see `HYPOTHESIS_REGISTER.md` H6. |
| Can competitors copy it? | UNTESTED — no moat analysis has been done yet (Gate 5/14). |

## Verdict for Cycle 1
The thesis is plausible and worth continuing to test, but it is currently a hypothesis
resting on almost no direct evidence of its hardest claims (performance-based pricing,
attribution, gainshare). The strongest asset this project has — AMOR 2020 — is evidence for
the easier, already-proven part of the ladder (cost-out, integration, contractor
consolidation), not the harder, more valuable part (performance partnership). See
`03_STRATEGIC_THESIS/red_team_review.md` for the sharpest version of this critique.
