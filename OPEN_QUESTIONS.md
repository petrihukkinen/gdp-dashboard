## Q1 — What actually happened to the 2020 AMOR proposal? [BLOCKING]
Was AMOR Oy formed? Did Neste approve or reject the MBO? If it proceeded, what were the
actual realized numbers against the €129m baseline, the €43m 5-year target, and the 350→310
FTE plan? This is the single highest-value fact available to this project and is currently
unknown. It determines whether AMOR 2030 is (a) reviving a shelved concept, (b) generalizing
a proven case, or (c) learning from a failure. **Needs Petri's direct answer before Gate 1
work should be treated as final.**

## Q2 — Who is the acting party for AMOR 2030?
Is this a strategy for an existing large industrial services company (e.g. Bilfinger, per
the brief's quality bar) to build internally, a new venture Petri would lead, or a
generalized concept not yet attached to a specific company? This changes the balance-sheet,
moat, and "why this company and not a competitor" sections substantially. See `ASSUMPTIONS.md`
AS2/AS4.

## Q3 — Is the customer base refining-only or broader heavy industry?
The 2020 source is 100% refinery-specific (Neste, Kilpilahti). The brief's language
("refinery or chemical site executive") suggests broader heavy-process industry. Confirm the
intended addressable market before value pool sizing is finalized.

## Q4 — What is the real target for "value pool" credibility: the €43m committed number or the €24-62m "potential" range?
The source document itself shows a large, unreconciled gap between the committed 5-year
target (€43m cumulative, or ~€17m/yr by year 5) and the sum of "potential" value pool
estimates (€48-62m/yr) on slide 22 — and slide 24 admits due diligence was never performed
on any of these figures. AMOR 2030 needs a position on which number is credible and why, or
it inherits the same credibility gap. See `05_VALUE_POOLS/value_pool_tree.md`.

## Q5 — Does Petri want AMOR 2030 to preserve the MBO/spinout structure, or move fully to a
performance-contract model delivered by an existing large provider?
These are structurally different businesses (see the three architecture options in
`03_STRATEGIC_THESIS/business_model_options.md`) with different capital, risk, and scale
profiles. The original AMOR was an MBO; the working hypothesis pyramid in the brief describes
an evolution path for an existing provider's client relationships, not a spinout model.
