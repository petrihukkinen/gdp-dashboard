# Customer CFO Validation Protocol — 30 Minutes

**Revised, Cycle 10.** Supersedes the Cycle 9 version, which tested willingness to pay a
*separate* Control Tower fee — a framing retired by the Accountability Economics reframe.
This version tests scope consolidation, duration, and accountability economics instead.
Problem interview, not a sales interview. Never ask "would you buy this."

## Q1 — Scope-consolidation baseline pain
*"How many separate maintenance/service providers do you currently manage at this site, and
what does managing that many relationships actually cost you in internal time and
coordination?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** whether fragmentation is real, costly pain (precondition for
  Mechanism 1).
- **Positive:** many providers, real, quantifiable coordination cost.
- **Warning:** some friction but manageable.
- **Kill:** no meaningful cost perceived from managing multiple providers.

## Q2 — Willingness to consolidate scope
*"If one provider could credibly take on a much larger share of that total scope — with
transparent governance and a real accountability mechanism — would that be attractive in
principle, or does keeping multiple providers matter to you for its own reasons (competitive
tension, risk diversification)?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** Mechanism 1's kill condition directly.
- **Positive:** open to consolidation in principle.
- **Warning:** conditional, wants to retain some multi-provider structure regardless.
- **Kill:** actively prefers fragmentation for competitive/risk-diversification reasons —
  the strongest possible falsification of Mechanism 1.

## Q3 — Contract-duration appetite
*"What contract duration would you consider normal for a broad-scope maintenance/services
relationship, and what would need to be true for you to go longer than your current norm?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** Mechanism 2.
- **Positive:** open to 5+ years given the right conditions, names specific conditions.
- **Warning:** open in theory, but the conditions named are very hard to meet.
- **Kill:** would never exceed current short-cycle norms regardless of conditions.

## Q4 — Rebid policy reality
*"Does your procurement policy require periodic competitive rebidding regardless of
incumbent performance, or is renewal genuinely open to negotiation based on track record?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** whether Duration Economics is capped by policy independent of
  relationship quality — Red Team item 3.
- **Positive:** genuine, performance-linked renewal flexibility exists.
- **Warning:** rebid is required but incumbents have real practical advantages.
- **Kill:** hard mandatory rebid regardless of performance — a structural ceiling on
  Mechanism 2 no amount of relationship quality can remove.

## Q5 — Customer's own concentration-risk concern
*"How concerned would you be about supplier concentration risk — one provider responsible
for a much larger share of your maintenance spend than today?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** the customer-side mirror of the provider's own concentration risk.
- **Positive:** manageable concern, outweighed by coordination benefits.
- **Warning:** real concern, would want specific mitigations.
- **Kill:** concentration risk alone would block consolidation regardless of value case.

## Q6 — Transparency requirements
*"What transparency would you need — data ownership, KPI history, documented processes —
before you'd feel comfortable with that level of concentration?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** whether `EARNED_RETENTION_PROTOCOL.md`'s protections are the right
  ones, and sufficient.
- **Positive:** names conditions the protocol already covers.
- **Warning:** names additional conditions not yet designed for.
- **Kill:** no transparency measures would be sufficient to offset the concentration concern.

## Q7 — Downside-accountability credibility
*"If a provider's own failure caused a major loss, and their financial consequence was
capped, would you find that credible as accountability, or would you want something else —
a bigger cap, insurance backing, personal guarantees?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** Event 4's credibility as the anchor of the whole Accountability
  Economics claim.
- **Positive:** the capped mechanism, as designed, is seen as credible.
- **Warning:** wants a materially different structure but is not fundamentally opposed.
- **Kill:** sees it as inadequate regardless of design — undermines the entire thesis's
  credibility anchor, not just Event 4.

## Q8 — Rebid/switching logic
*"When this contract comes up for renewal, what would make you switch providers versus
renew — and would switching actually be practical given how integrated the relationship
might become?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** earned retention versus lock-in, from the customer's own mouth.
- **Positive:** names performance-based switching criteria; believes switching would remain
  practical.
- **Warning:** names switching criteria but is unsure practicality holds.
- **Kill:** believes switching would become impractical regardless of performance — a
  lock-in signal, not an earned-retention one.

## Q9 — Total-cost versus unit-rate procurement logic
*"Does your organization evaluate maintenance/services spend on total cost of the
relationship, or on unit rates — and would a broader, longer relationship need to prove
itself on the same unit-rate basis?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** a previously untested procurement-mechanics question — whether the
  ALV logic (`ACCOUNT_LIFETIME_VALUE_MODEL.md`) is even the basis on which this gets
  evaluated internally, or whether unit-rate scrutiny would override it.
- **Positive:** total-cost/relationship-value evaluation is accepted practice.
- **Warning:** unit-rate scrutiny dominates but total-cost arguments can supplement it.
- **Kill:** procurement will only ever evaluate on unit rates — the whole ALV argument would
  never reach the actual decision-maker in a form they'd act on.

## Q10 — Pull-through trust
*"If this provider also identified and proposed additional project or turnaround work
through the same relationship, would you want that competitively tendered every time, or
would you trust the relationship enough to sole-source it under some circumstances?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** Mechanism 4, and whether `PULL_THROUGH_CONFLICT_PROTOCOL.md`'s
  safeguards are sufficient or overkill.
- **Positive:** willing to sole-source under defined conditions.
- **Warning:** wants tendering for everything above a low threshold.
- **Kill:** would always require full competitive tender regardless of relationship —
  caps Mechanism 4 to lead-generation value only.

## Q11 — Internal budget ownership
*"Which budget or organizational owner would need to approve a multi-year, broad-scope
commitment like this?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** practical approval-path reality.
- **Positive:** a clear, identifiable owner and process.
- **Warning:** unclear, would require new approval infrastructure.
- **Kill:** no plausible approval path exists at all.

## Q12 — Closing: does the qualitative case need a hard number?
*"If everything else were credible — governance, accountability, transparency — would
broader scope and longer duration alone be enough reason to consolidate with one provider,
or would you still need a specific cost or performance number to justify it?"*
- **Hypothesis tested:** whether the Accountability Economics case can be sold qualitatively
  or requires hard ALV-style numbers before any commercial conversation can proceed.
- **Positive:** the qualitative case (governance, transparency, accountability) could carry
  a serious conversation.
- **Warning:** qualitative case opens the door, but a number will ultimately be required.
- **Kill:** no conversation proceeds without hard numbers upfront — meaning real cost/
  benefit data must exist before this thesis can be pitched at all, not just before it can
  be closed.
