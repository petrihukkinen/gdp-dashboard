# Customer CFO Validation Protocol — 30 Minutes

Problem interview, not a sales interview. Do not describe AMOR before asking these
questions — lead with their reality, not the pitch. Never ask "would you buy this."

## Q1 — Opening (fragmented accountability, problem-first)
*"Walk me through how your organization currently manages accountability across multiple
maintenance contractors, OEMs, and internal teams on a live production issue — who is
actually in charge when something goes wrong at 2am?"*
- **Testing:** whether fragmented accountability is a real, costly problem at all.
- **Positive signal:** describes real confusion, multiple escalation paths, no single owner.
- **Warning signal:** "it's fine, we have a strong internal PM function."
- **Kill signal:** has never been a problem worth solving.

## Q2 — Willingness to pay for orchestration
*"If a single external party took formal responsibility for orchestrating that entire
ecosystem — but you still paid every contractor and OEM the same as today — would that
coordination role itself be something you'd pay a separate, visible fee for, or would you
expect it bundled invisibly into existing contracts?"*
- **Testing:** Falsification Register #1, directly.
- **Positive:** open to a visible, separate fee, can articulate its worth.
- **Warning:** "maybe, but it would need to offset headcount cost we already carry."
- **Kill:** "we'd never pay extra for that — that's just what a competent contractor does."

## Q3 — Base Fee credibility
*"How is your current maintenance base fee structured — how much of it, if any, is
explicitly for management/coordination versus hands-on labor and materials?"*
- **Testing:** whether a "management layer" fee has any precedent in their world.
- **Positive:** can point to an existing management-fee component.
- **Warning:** everything is blended, no visibility into it.
- **Kill:** any attempt to separate a "management fee" would be rejected by procurement as
  padding.

## Q4 — Downside corridor credibility
*"If a provider's own maintenance failure caused a documented production loss, and the
contract capped the provider's financial consequence at a fixed percentage of the annual
fee — does that cap feel like real accountability, or a ceiling that protects the provider
more than it protects you?"*
- **Testing:** Falsification Register #2.
- **Positive:** sees the cap as a meaningful, bounded commitment.
- **Warning:** wants a materially higher cap or no cap at all.
- **Kill:** sees it as worse than standard liability clauses already give them.

## Q5 — Attribution confidence
*"Who in your organization would need to sign off on classifying a production loss as
'caused by the maintenance provider' versus 'caused by something else' — and would you trust
a joint board, with equal votes from both sides, to make that call?"*
- **Testing:** attribution confidence and Value Validation Board credibility.
- **Positive:** names a plausible owner, open to joint governance.
- **Warning:** names real internal political difficulty.
- **Kill:** would never accept a joint decision — attribution must be unilaterally theirs.

## Q6 — Value validation burden
*"How much staff time could your Finance and Operations teams realistically commit each
month to reviewing and validating claims under a performance-based contract?"*
- **Testing:** Falsification Register #9, from the customer side.
- **Positive:** names a credible, nonzero capacity.
- **Warning:** very limited capacity, wants something largely automated.
- **Kill:** essentially zero appetite for any review burden.

## Q7 — Existing budget ownership
*"Which budget line currently owns contractor management and coordination costs — would a
Control Tower fee come out of that budget, a new budget, or displace existing headcount?"*
- **Testing:** procurement/budget-ownership reality.
- **Positive:** a clear existing budget line it could map to.
- **Warning:** unclear, would need new budget approval.
- **Kill:** no such budget exists and creating one is organizationally infeasible.

## Q8 — Double-payment concern
*"If a contractor productivity improvement is already assumed inside your Base Fee
calculation, and the provider also asks for a share of that improvement as a separate
payment — does that feel like paying twice for the same thing?"*
- **Testing:** the core objection the Contractor Productivity Value Module must survive.
- **Positive:** understands the distinction once the baseline-vs-improvement logic is
  explained.
- **Warning:** skeptical, needs significant convincing.
- **Kill:** flatly calls any version of this double-dipping, non-negotiable.

## Q9 — Contractor productivity economics
*"If we showed you a signed contractor invoice showing a rate reduction against a
documented prior baseline, would Finance validate and pay a formula-based share of that
saving without case-by-case renegotiation?"*
- **Testing:** Falsification Register #6, concretely.
- **Positive:** yes, with an agreed formula.
- **Warning:** yes, but wants case-by-case re-approval every time (defeats the design).
- **Kill:** no — any variable payment tied to their own invoices is unacceptable.

## Q10 — Avoided-loss economics
*"Would you pay for a demonstrated avoided production loss — something that, by definition,
didn't happen — or does that feel fundamentally different from paying for a cost reduction
you can see on an invoice?"*
- **Testing:** whether Cycle 7's kill of avoided-value monetization was the right call, or
  whether real customers are actually more receptive than the internal Red Team assumed.
- **Positive:** open to it with strong evidence.
- **Warning:** skeptical but not fully closed.
- **Kill:** flatly rejects paying for a counterfactual under any circumstances — would
  confirm the Cycle 7 kill decision was correct, not just internally reasoned.

## Q11 — Procurement barriers
*"What would your procurement function's biggest objection be to a contract with a
variable, performance-linked payment component instead of a single fixed price?"*
- **Testing:** real procurement barriers.
- **Positive:** names a specific, addressable objection.
- **Warning:** names a structural policy barrier (e.g. "we're not permitted to do variable
  pricing").
- **Kill:** procurement would block this outright with no viable path around it.

## Q12 — Closing: core-only viability
*"If none of the value-sharing modules applied to your site — just a base fee for
orchestration and a capped accountability clause — would that still be worth a serious
conversation, or does the value only exist if the upside modules are active?"*
- **Testing:** Falsification Register #10 — the single most important closing signal.
- **Positive:** yes, the core alone is genuinely interesting.
- **Warning:** "only if" conditions attached.
- **Kill:** no — without upside modules there's nothing here worth discussing.
