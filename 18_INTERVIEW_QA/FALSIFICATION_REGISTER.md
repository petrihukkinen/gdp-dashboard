# Falsification Register — Cycle 9

Ten assumptions which, if proven false by real external evidence, would materially damage
or kill the current settled architecture (Base-Fee-funded Control Tower + Event 4 downside
corridor, core; Contractor Productivity Value Module and Turnaround Value Module, optional).
No claim of validation is made here — this is the register of what still needs to be learned.

## CRITICAL

### 1. Customers will pay a distinguishable fee for orchestration itself
- **Current assumption:** the Control Tower's orchestration/accountability role has
  standalone commercial value a customer will pay for as such, not just expect bundled into
  delivery pricing.
- **Why it matters:** this is the foundation of the entire "core" recommendation from Cycle
  8 — if false, there is no Base Fee to fund the Control Tower with.
- **Evidence available:** none externally. AMOR 2020 bundled everything into one maintenance
  fee (`EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` A7/A8) and never tested a separate orchestration fee. ABB's
  Full Service contracts (Level B) were fixed-price delivery contracts, not orchestration-fee
  contracts.
- **Evidence missing:** any customer's actual willingness to pay a distinguishable fee for
  coordination/accountability versus expecting it "free" from a competent provider.
- **Who can validate:** customer CFOs, procurement leaders.
- **Supports the model:** customers articulate real coordination pain and would pay to
  remove it as a distinct line item.
- **Weakens:** customers would only pay for it bundled invisibly into delivery pricing.
- **Kills:** customers say orchestration has zero standalone value — it's the provider's own
  operating cost, not a commercial conversation.

### 2. Event 4's downside corridor is credible, differentiated accountability
- **Current assumption:** a capped, provider-funded painshare mechanism is seen as real
  differentiation, not a repackaged liability clause.
- **Why it matters:** Cycle 8 found Event 4, not Event 1, is what prevents regression to
  "Better Managed Maintenance" — if this assumption fails, the core has no differentiator.
- **Evidence available:** none — Cycle 8's own Task 8 already flagged the absence of
  benchmark evidence.
- **Evidence missing:** whether customers/lawyers see this as materially different from
  bonus/malus, SLA credits, or liquidated damages they already have.
- **Who can validate:** customer CFOs, customer contract counsel, industrial site directors.
- **Supports:** described as going meaningfully beyond standard terms.
- **Weakens:** seen as marginally different.
- **Kills:** seen as indistinguishable from, or worse than, terms they'd negotiate directly.

### 3. Customers will cede genuine orchestration/decision authority, not just pay for it
- **Current assumption:** customers will grant real coordination authority over their
  contractor ecosystem (selection influence, prioritization, direction), not just fund a
  fee.
- **Why it matters:** Control Tower Economics is meaningless in practice without real
  authority, independent of whether the fee itself is accepted (#1 is commercial, this is
  operational).
- **Evidence available:** AMOR 2020 shows the opposite pattern under real trust-based
  conditions — Neste retained budget-approval rights, spares ownership, and a reversion
  clause even in a founder-led MBO (`EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` A3, A4, A7).
- **Evidence missing:** whether a formal, arms-length contract (not a trust-based MBO) would
  cede more or less authority than AMOR 2020 did.
- **Who can validate:** asset directors, maintenance directors, site directors.
- **Supports:** willingness to delegate a specific, meaningful set of decisions.
- **Weakens:** willingness limited to a narrow list that guts the orchestration role.
- **Kills:** no delegation acceptable under any framing — echoes AMOR 2020's own retained-
  control pattern, this time as a structural rule, not a one-off design choice.

### 4. The provider organization will not internally block this
- **Current assumption:** a Bilfinger-scale provider's existing sales, account-ownership,
  and P&L structures will not resist a model that changes who sells, who owns the customer,
  who carries risk, and who gets revenue/margin credit.
- **Why it matters:** never tested at all — a real risk that could kill the model regardless
  of customer receptiveness.
- **Evidence available:** none.
- **Evidence missing:** internal organizational reaction from the specific functions whose
  incentives this model disrupts.
- **Who can validate:** provider CFO/CEO, division/regional P&L owners.
- **Supports:** clear, non-contentious answers to who-sells/who-owns/who-carries-risk/who-
  gets-credit.
- **Weakens:** answers exist but create real cross-functional friction.
- **Kills:** no workable answer exists, or the answer triggers organizationally fatal
  resistance.

### 5. Control Tower Economics is a systematized capability, not relabeled contract management
- **Current assumption:** the attribution engine, decision-rights architecture, and joint
  governance constitute a genuinely new, hard-to-replicate capability.
- **Why it matters:** this is the entire strategic-differentiation claim — see
  `03_STRATEGIC_THESIS/DIFFERENTIATION_FALSIFICATION.md`.
- **Evidence available:** none demonstrating non-replicability; the mechanics (caps, joint
  board, bounded productivity share) are governance/contracting choices a sophisticated
  competitor could copy within one sales cycle, not a technology or data asset.
- **Evidence missing:** any cross-site or multi-customer evidence that the architecture gets
  cheaper or more valuable to replicate as it scales (the only real moat candidate).
- **Who can validate:** cannot be validated by a single interview — requires multi-customer,
  multi-year evidence.
- **Supports:** customers/competitors describe this as genuinely novel.
- **Weakens:** described as a smarter version of existing integrated-maintenance practice.
- **Kills:** competitors are already doing functionally the same thing under different
  language.

## HIGH

### 6. The Contractor Productivity Value Module's bounded design solves the fairness objection
- **Current assumption:** the Cycle 8 redesign (time-limited share, rolling baseline) is
  seen by customers as fair, not as "paying twice."
- **Evidence available:** internal analytical reasoning only (Cycle 8).
- **Evidence missing:** real customer reaction to the specific bounded mechanic.
- **Who can validate:** customer CFOs and Finance functions.
- **Supports/weakens/kills:** see `CUSTOMER_CFO_VALIDATION.md` Q8-9.

### 7. ABB experience generalizes to this architecture, not just to fixed-price transition
- **Current assumption:** Petri's ABB experience supports the full atomic-event/attribution
  architecture, not only the narrower claim that fixed-price/performance-linked maintenance
  is achievable.
- **Evidence available:** Level B — real, but general (`PETRI_EXPERIENCE.md` B1).
- **Evidence missing:** whether any of the 43 contracts included anything resembling joint
  governance, capped painshare, or bounded productivity-sharing mechanics.
- **Who can validate:** Petri's own recollection of specific contract structures (a bounded,
  internal validation step, not external).
- See `PETRI_ARCHITECTURE_EVIDENCE_MAP.md` for the full mapping.

### 8. A real joint Value Validation Board will be accepted, not just designed
- **Current assumption:** a skeptical site director will accept a joint board with real
  adjudication power over money.
- **Evidence missing:** any real reaction to ceding unilateral attribution authority.
- **Who can validate:** site directors, asset directors.

### 9. Governance burden will not be perceived as disproportionate
- **Current assumption:** Cycles 5-6's internally-modeled bureaucracy burden (13-15 claims/
  year, ~25-40/year break point) will not feel disproportionate to real customer Finance/Ops
  teams.
- **Evidence missing:** real capacity and tolerance data.
- **Who can validate:** customer CFO and Finance functions.

## MEDIUM

### 10. "Core Service without Event 1" is sellable as a complete offering, not a downgrade
- **Current assumption:** customers will accept the core (Control Tower + Event 4) alone as
  a complete, credible product when a customer doesn't qualify for or want the optional
  modules.
- **Evidence missing:** whether "no upside module" reads as complete or as an inferior,
  stripped-down version of something better.
- **Who can validate:** all three stakeholder categories — see the closing question in each
  validation protocol.
