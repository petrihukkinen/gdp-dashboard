# Systematic Capability Map — Cycle 10

Reframed question, per instruction: not "is the Control Tower differentiated," but **what
systematic capability would let a provider repeatedly win and profitably operate broader
accountability relationships better than competitors?**

| Capability | Category | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Customer dependency taxonomy (permit timing, production access, approved vendor lists, etc., as named exclusions) | **TABLE STAKES** | Sophisticated contract negotiators already carve these out. This project's contribution is rigor and consistency, not novelty. |
| Standardized transition methodology | **ADVANTAGE** | Most competitors likely improvise transitions; a documented, repeatable methodology is a real execution edge — but observable and copyable once demonstrated, not IP-protected. |
| Accountability boundary architecture (`ACCOUNTABILITY_BOUNDARY.md`'s six-way split) | **ADVANTAGE** | Reduces legal/commercial risk and is likely more precise than common practice — but a competitor's counsel could replicate the framework quickly once it sees it. |
| Governance (Value Validation Board, joint decision rights) | **ADVANTAGE** | Genuinely more rigorous than typical, but joint governance boards are not a unique concept — adoptable by any competitor willing to build the discipline. |
| Risk corridor design (caps, collars, clawback) | **ADVANTAGE, bordering TABLE STAKES over time** | A competent implementation of capped-liability structures that already exist elsewhere in industrial contracting — not an invention. |
| Scalable modular discipline (the willingness to make Contractor Productivity / Turnaround modules optional, not force them on every customer) | **ADVANTAGE** | The discipline of pruning rather than overselling complexity (Cycles 6-8's hard-won simplification) is real and likely uncommon — but again copyable once observed. |
| Control Tower operating system (the attribution engine, decision trees, governance processes built across Cycles 2-9) | **POTENTIAL MOAT, UNPROVEN** | The most genuinely differentiated artifact this project has built. Its value as a moat depends entirely on whether it can be operated efficiently and replicated cheaply across many sites (`FIVE_ECONOMIC_MECHANISMS.md` Mechanism 5) — unproven at even a second site. |
| Cross-site performance data (benchmarking, baselines, failure-mode libraries that compound across customers) | **POTENTIAL MOAT, UNPROVEN** | The strongest theoretical moat candidate identified since Cycle 9 — and still entirely unbuilt, since it requires multiple customer relationships and time neither of which exist yet. |
| Sustained commercial discipline as a market reputation (walking away from bad-fit customers, killing weak mechanisms rather than overselling them) | **UNPROVEN, long-horizon** | Speculative — if this discipline persists into real delivery and becomes visible to the market over years, it could become a genuine reputational moat. Not testable now. |

## Summary
**TABLE STAKES:** customer dependency taxonomy.
**ADVANTAGE (real today, but replicable once observed):** transition methodology,
accountability boundary architecture, governance, risk corridor design, modular discipline.
**POTENTIAL MOAT (unproven, requires time and multiple customers to test):** the Control
Tower operating system's cost-to-replicate at scale; cross-site performance data;
reputation for commercial discipline.

None of the "ADVANTAGE" items should be presented as a moat — they are real but copyable
execution quality. The only genuine moat candidates require evidence this project cannot
generate: operating the model at more than one site, over time. This is consistent with,
and does not resolve, the Cycle 9 Differentiation Falsification verdict of UNRESOLVED.
