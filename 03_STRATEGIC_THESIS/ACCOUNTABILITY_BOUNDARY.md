# Accountability Boundary — Cycle 10

Attacking "single accountable provider." Accountable for *what*, exactly? Six distinct
accountability types must never be collapsed into one phrase.

| Accountability type | Owner | Delegable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery Accountability** — executing the specific maintenance work performed directly, to spec and schedule | Provider | Yes — this is the core of the service itself |
| **Orchestration Accountability** — coordinating, scheduling, and holding other parties (subcontractors, OEMs) to their commitments within the managed ecosystem | Provider | Yes, bounded by named customer dependencies (approved vendor lists, procurement terms — the "primarily provider-controlled, with defined customer dependencies" tagging established since Cycle 2/5) |
| **Performance Accountability** — the capped financial consequence for demonstrable production loss the provider directly causes through its own maintenance failure | Provider | Yes, but strictly bounded — Event 4's corridor, not open-ended |
| **Asset Owner Accountability** — ultimate responsibility for asset condition, investment decisions (Capex), and overall risk posture | **Customer** | **No — never delegable**, regardless of relationship depth |
| **Statutory Accountability** — regulatory, environmental, and process-safety compliance obligations | **Customer** | **No — a legal fact under virtually all industrial regulatory regimes, not a negotiable design choice.** The provider can be a delivery partner in compliance activities; it does not inherit the legal accountability |
| **Production Accountability** — decisions about how to operate the asset (rates, feedstock, campaigns) and the resulting production outcomes | **Customer** | **No.** This is precisely why Event 4's decision tree excludes operations-driven and envelope-breach scenarios (Steps 3.5, 3.6, 4) |

## Standing rule
"Single accountable provider" is dangerous language and must be qualified every time it is
used in any sales, positioning, or contractual context — it must never be allowed to imply
the provider inherits asset ownership, statutory compliance, or production accountability.
This is not a one-time clarification; it is a permanent constraint on how AMOR 2030 may be
described, on the same footing as `CLAUDE.md`'s other non-negotiable rules.

## One-sentence accountability boundary
**AMOR is accountable for what it delivers and what it orchestrates, within a capped
financial consequence for its own failures — never for the customer's asset, its
compliance obligations, or its production decisions.**
