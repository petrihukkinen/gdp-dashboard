# Control Tower Economics

Core AMOR 2030 design principle, named and adopted per Petri's direct instruction
(2026-07-11). Status: Level C (strategic design hypothesis), grounded in Level A evidence
from AMOR 2020's own ecosystem design.

## The principle

The provider does not earn its margin primarily by performing maintenance labor. It earns
its margin by **owning planning, orchestration, performance visibility, and accountability**
across an ecosystem it does not fully staff itself:

- Own personnel (direct delivery, highest control)
- Main service providers / key subcontracting partners (deep, long-term relationships)
- Specialist contractors (narrow scope, transactional)
- OEMs (equipment-specific, often the customer's own long-term relationship)
- Turnaround resources (surge capacity, typically the highest-risk category)
- Reliability experts (engineering discipline, may be internal or partnered)
- Digital/data providers (CMMS, condition monitoring, analytics platforms)

This is a materially different value proposition than "we are your maintenance contractor."
It is closer to a systems-integrator or general-contractor model than a labor-supply model —
the provider's differentiated asset is the orchestration layer, not the wrench-turning
capacity.

## Evidence this isn't invented from nothing

AMOR 2020 already designed something structurally close to this, without naming it:
`EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` A13 — AMOR positioned as "integrator," a 300-FTE core surrounded by
tiered Main Service Provider rings (150 FTE, 125 FTE) and an Other Service Providers ring
(25 FTE), managing roughly 600 FTE of total ecosystem capacity through a 300-FTE core. AMOR
2020 built the *shape* of Control Tower Economics (source PDF, slide 17) but never built the
*commercial* logic for it — the earnings model (A7, A8) is still priced as if AMOR were
simply performing (or subcontracting) work, not paid for orchestration and accountability as
a distinct, priced capability.

## What AMOR 2030 must do that AMOR 2020 didn't

1. **Price the orchestration layer explicitly**, not just the labor and subcontract pass-
   through. A Control Tower fee or margin structure distinct from delivery fees.
2. **Make planning, performance visibility, and accountability into deliverables with their
   own KPIs** — e.g. schedule-adherence-across-the-ecosystem, contractor-performance
   dashboard completeness/timeliness, cross-provider root-cause coordination — not just
   labor-cost KPIs.
3. **Be honest about where orchestration control ends.** Control Tower Economics is a
   stronger role than "maintenance contractor," but it is not full control — see the Pool 1
   correction in `05_VALUE_POOLS/value_pool_tree.md` ("primarily provider-controlled, with
   defined customer dependencies"). The Control Tower orchestrates; it does not unilaterally
   decide supplier lists, Capex, or work permits. Overclaiming control here is the same
   painshare risk flagged in `RISK_REGISTER.md`.
4. **Digital/data providers belong in the ecosystem ring, not bolted on as an afterthought**
   — AMOR 2020 treated CMMS as a single, unsized bullet (`05_VALUE_POOLS/value_pool_tree.md`
   Pool 5). A real Control Tower needs its data/digital layer to be a first-class ecosystem
   member, because performance visibility is the product.

## Why this matters for the business model architecture

Control Tower Economics is the design principle underneath the revised recommendation in
`business_model_options.md`: it's what makes "Asset Performance Partnership" (market entry)
credible as a step toward "Selective Outcome-Based Asset Performance Platform" (destination)
rather than just a bigger version of "Managed Maintenance Transformation." A pure labor/
subcontract-delivery business does not naturally evolve into an outcome-based platform — an
orchestration/accountability business does, because orchestration is what performance
attribution and gainshare actually require.

**Not yet done:** sizing what a Control Tower fee/margin actually looks like commercially,
and testing whether customers will pay for orchestration as a distinct line item versus
expecting it bundled into delivery pricing. Flagged for Gate 3 commercial modelling, not
fabricated here.
