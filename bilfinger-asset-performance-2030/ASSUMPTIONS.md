# Assumptions Register

Living document. Every assumption below is tagged by type and by how it would be invalidated. Update this file whenever a new deliverable relies on something not already listed here, or whenever an assumption is confirmed/refuted.

Legend: **[FACT]** sourced and dated · **[EXPERIENCE]** Petri's first-hand, lived knowledge · **[HYPOTHESIS]** reasoned but unvalidated

---

## About Bilfinger

1. **[FACT]** Bilfinger's FY2025 EBITA margin was 5.5%, and its stated 2030 ambition is 8–9%. Source: `14_RESEARCH/BILFINGER_FACTS.md`.
2. **[FACT]** Bilfinger already brands a performance/outcome positioning ("Your Performance Is Our Business") and operates a named digital platform (BCAP®). Source: same.
3. **[HYPOTHESIS]** The 8–9% EBITA ambition cannot be reached through labour-rate delivery alone and requires a materially richer service/margin mix — this is inferred from the scale of the gap, not confirmed by any Bilfinger capital-markets-day statement we've sourced. **Invalidated if:** Bilfinger's own investor materials show the 2030 margin target is expected to come primarily from portfolio/geographic mix shift or M&A rather than service-model evolution.
4. **[HYPOTHESIS]** No Bilfinger business unit currently runs a KPI-linked or gainshare commercial contract with a named customer at scale. Based on an absence of evidence in public search results, not a confirmed negative. **Invalidated if:** Petri or a Bilfinger insider identifies an existing gainshare/painshare contract already in the portfolio (which would change this from "new concept" to "scale existing capability").
5. **[HYPOTHESIS]** Bilfinger's regional P&L and sales-incentive structures are currently built around scope/rate-card economics, making a KPI-fee model an internal change-management challenge, not just a customer-facing one. Unconfirmed — no visibility into Bilfinger's internal incentive design.
6. **[HYPOTHESIS]** "BCAP®" is technically capable of supplying much of the data infrastructure an attribution model would need. Unconfirmed — public material describes monitoring/alerting/predictive features, not confirmed access to production-planning or operations-loss-classification data specifically.

## About the customer / market

7. **[EXPERIENCE]** Asset owners (Neste-side experience) are structurally cost-center-incentivized on maintenance spend even when reliability underperformance costs them far more — this is a lived pattern, not a universal claim about every industrial company.
8. **[HYPOTHESIS]** Continuous-process industries (refining, petrochemicals) are the most tractable first vertical for attribution because of instrumentation density and loss-event tagging maturity, relative to discrete manufacturing or less-instrumented sectors. Reasoned from AMOR/Neste experience; not tested outside refining.
9. **[HYPOTHESIS]** Most industrial procurement/legal functions are not currently built to buy outcome-based contracts and will resist this model regardless of technical merit. Based on general industry pattern-matching (performance-contracting literature, e.g., Simon-Kucher's outcomes-based-contracting research cited in `14_RESEARCH/`) rather than direct evidence about specific target accounts.

## About the concept itself

10. **[HYPOTHESIS]** A Value Validation Board, properly resourced, can produce attribution rulings that both a customer and Bilfinger will honor without recurring disputes escalating to contract termination. This is the single largest unvalidated assumption in the entire concept — see `RISK_REGISTER.md` item #1 and `STRATEGIC_THESIS.md` Section 4.
11. **[HYPOTHESIS]** Painshare exposure can be structured (capped, narrow-scope) so that it does not create a balance-sheet/contingent-liability problem Bilfinger's CFO would reject outright. Not yet reviewed by anyone with Bilfinger finance/treasury visibility.
12. **[HYPOTHESIS]** A pilot account exists (or can be found) with sufficient data quality and existing relationship depth to make Phase 0 due diligence produce a usable attribution baseline within a reasonable timeframe (3–4 months). Not yet identified against an actual account list — Bilfinger's real account portfolio and data maturity is unknown to this project.
13. **[HYPOTHESIS]** 10–20% of total contract fee is a workable illustrative size for the Phase 3 performance-fee band — a placeholder for modeling, not a validated number. See `08_FINANCIAL_MODEL/` (to be built in cycle two).

## About Petri's positioning

14. **[EXPERIENCE]** The combination of ABB (provider-side performance contracting) + Neste (owner-side asset management) + AMOR (integrated operating-model design) is a genuinely rare combination for pricing this specific risk. This is a defensible claim about combination-rarity, not a claim that no one else has any one of these experiences individually.
15. **[HYPOTHESIS]** This combination is what a Bilfinger hiring panel is actually looking for, as opposed to, e.g., a pure digital/technology background or a pure large-account sales background. Unconfirmed — depends on the actual role specification and panel priorities, which this project has not been given visibility into.
