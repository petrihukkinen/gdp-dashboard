# Risk Register — Bilfinger Asset Performance 2030

Living document, maker–checker discipline applies. Each risk has an ID, category, severity (impact if realized), likelihood (current, first-cycle judgment), and mitigation/status. Severity and likelihood are qualitative (High/Medium/Low) — this is a strategy-stage register, not a quantified project risk log; quantification comes in `08_FINANCIAL_MODEL/` once the concept is more mature.

| ID | Category | Risk | Severity | Likelihood | Mitigation / current status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | KPI attribution | No proven methodology exists to split maintenance-caused vs. other losses in a live, adversarial (real-money) commercial setting. AMOR never built this; no confirmed Bilfinger precedent found. | High | High | Phase-gated commercial model (only fee-link what's attributable); Value Validation Board designed as joint governance; **not yet validated with a real disputed case** — top priority for cycle two (see WORKPLAN.md). |
| R2 | Gainshare verification | Baselines are gameable both directions — customer fears inflated baseline (pays for nothing), Bilfinger fears deflated baseline (can never beat it) or shifting baselines (moving goalposts). | High | High | Phase 0 due-diligence gate before any KPI/gainshare phase; joint (not unilateral) baseline-setting; explicit re-basing triggers for scope/feedstock/asset changes. Mechanism named, not yet contract-drafted. |
| R3 | Balance sheet / financial reporting | Painshare commitments can create contingent liabilities Bilfinger's CFO/auditors must reserve against or disclose, capping how much painshare is realistically acceptable. | Medium-High | Medium | Design principle: painshare capped as % of fixed fee, narrow-scope only (Bilfinger-attributable KPIs). Not yet reviewed by anyone with Bilfinger finance/treasury visibility — open question #16. |
| R4 | Commercial credibility / differentiation | Bilfinger already publicly claims "Your Performance Is Our Business" and runs BCAP® — pitching "performance orientation" as a new idea would look uninformed. | Medium | High (if positioning isn't corrected) | Reposition explicitly as the commercial/contractual mechanism that operationalizes the existing slogan, not as a new strategic direction. Addressed in STRATEGIC_THESIS.md §0; must be consistently applied in every later deliverable (deck, pitch, interview answers). |
| R5 | Customer adoption | Procurement and legal functions at target customers are typically built around scope/rate-card RFPs, not outcome-based buying; deals can die in vendor-approval/legal-risk review regardless of technical merit. | Medium-High | Medium-High | Pilot approach targets existing accounts with established relationship and trust, reducing the "new vendor category" adoption barrier. Contract templates need co-design with Bilfinger legal early, not late. |
| R6 | Organizational resistance (internal) | A KPI-fee/gainshare model changes how regional P&L owners are measured and how sales is compensated — an internal change-management problem the current concept treats as customer-facing only. | Medium-High | Medium-High | Not yet addressed in the concept beyond acknowledgment. Needs an explicit internal-adoption workstream in `06_OPERATING_MODEL/` in cycle two. |
| R7 | Scalability | Attribution frameworks are plant-/customer-specific (instrumentation, process technology, asset age all differ); a framework proven at one site may not transfer cleanly, undermining the multi-customer/multi-country scaling story. | Medium-High | Medium-High | Scale the methodology/playbook and governance charter, not a bespoke per-account solution; sequence rollout by vertical tractability (continuous process industries first). Still largely aspirational — no second site has been tested even hypothetically. |
| R8 | Data ownership / availability | Attribution and any digital/AI module need production-planning, process-control, and reliability data the customer may treat as sensitive, may not have in usable form, or may not agree to share under the necessary terms. | High | Medium-High | Every proposed digital/AI capability must clear the discipline in `07_DIGITAL_AI/` (data required, ownership, availability) before inclusion. Not yet tested against a real customer's actual data environment. |
| R9 | Talent / capability gap | A credible, resourced Value Validation Board and attribution-methodology function doesn't exist inside a typical maintenance-services organisation and isn't free to build. | Medium | High | Needs headcount/cost modeling in `08_FINANCIAL_MODEL/`; currently unresourced in the concept — a real gap, not yet a plan. |
| R10 | Pilot selection | Choosing a pilot site/customer for enthusiasm or relationship warmth rather than data quality and attribution feasibility would produce a pilot that either fails outright or produces a non-generalizable case study. | Medium-High | Medium | Phase 0 gate explicitly includes a go/no-go on data quality, not just customer willingness. No actual pilot candidate identified yet — open question #7. |
| R11 | Contract term / lock-in risk | Multi-year phase-gated contracts (mirroring AMOR's 5-year term logic) reduce switching flexibility for the customer, which sophisticated procurement teams will negotiate hard against, potentially forcing shorter terms that undercut the "earn trust over time" logic the whole staged model depends on. | Medium | Medium | Needs contract-structuring input from Bilfinger legal; unresolved tension, not yet an answered question. |
| R12 | Reputational / precedent risk | If an early Phase 3/4 deal produces a public dispute (customer refuses to pay a claimed gainshare, or disputes a painshare charge), it could discredit the model for every subsequent sales conversation, not just that account. | High | Low-Medium (low until Phase 3/4 deals exist; rises directly with each additional deal) | Value Validation Board's dispute-resolution and escalation path (named third-party reliability auditor as tie-break) exists specifically to prevent disputes reaching this point; needs to be contractually binding, not just a stated intention. |
| R13 | Confidentiality / source-material risk | The AMOR deck is marked confidential/non-binding and names real individuals (Petri's former colleagues) and Neste-specific figures; using it as source material for an external pitch could create a confidentiality or relationship problem if handled carelessly. | Medium | Medium | Explicit open question to Petri (open question #5) on how much to name vs. anonymize/generalize before any of this goes external. Nothing in this project should be shared outside Petri's own use until that call is made. |

## Top risks carried into the executive summary

Ranked by (severity × likelihood × how load-bearing the risk is to the whole concept): **R1 (attribution methodology unproven)**, **R2 (baseline gaming)**, **R8 (data ownership/availability)**, and **R6 (internal Bilfinger resistance)** are the four risks that most determine whether this concept survives contact with a real deal. Everything else in this register is manageable with normal contract and program discipline; these four are not yet solved, only structured.
