# Source: AMOR Oy — "Asset Management One Refinery" Executive Summary Deck

Original file: draft_final.pdf (uploaded by Petri Hukkinen). Authors: Petri Hukkinen, Jari Ala-Nikkola, Mika Raassina, Juha Kekkonen. Marked confidential, non-binding, discussion purposes only. Dated to the H2/2020 planning horizon (references "2020 proposal", implementation from 2021).

Note on naming: the deck itself uses "OPP" as a placeholder in the summary sections but the later, detailed sections use the real name **Neste** and reference **One Refinery** (Neste's Porvoo/Kilpilahti and Naantali refining assets in Finland). Real named individuals and Neste's supplier names (Hampton, Stanley, Preston — likely aliased in the source or actual contractor names) appear in later slides. This transcript preserves the deck's own terminology per slide.

This transcript is a structured extraction, not a verbatim OCR dump — numbers, structure, and wording are preserved faithfully.

## 1. Executive summary (p.2)

New service company **AMOR Oy** proposed to serve One Refinery:
- AMOR Oy established by current MMA (Maintenance & Asset Management, presumed) management, if OPP/Neste pursues the option.
- OPP starts a partnership with AMOR Oy.
- OPP asset management activities and resources transferred to AMOR Oy.
- AMOR takes responsibility for asset management at One Refinery in Finland (same scope as MMA today).
- Stated benefits of collaboration:
  - Annual maintenance services ordered on the basis of need.
  - AMOR acts as integrator for the large subcontractor base.
  - Lower maintenance cost to OPP — annualized savings of up to €15m by 2025.
  - Restructuring of own organisation, higher efficiency.
  - Opportunity to rearrange union relations (service branch).
  - Opportunity for tailored, lower-cost systems, CMMS, and administration.
  - Option to add fire brigades (Porvoo and Naantali) into AMOR's scope.

## 3. Model for earnings (p.4)

- **Years 1–2:** Open book, markup on own cost. Support functions run in collaboration with Neste.
- **Years 3–5:** Fixed price for fixed scope, even monthly invoicing. Additional work outside fixed scope invoiced hourly. Investment/development projects invoiced on separate orders. Other Kilpilahti customers served per separate agreement.

## 4. Key performance indicators (p.5)

- Operational availability — rising trend target
- MTBR (mean time between repairs) for critical equipment — rising trend
- Customer satisfaction — rising trend
- TRIF (Total Recordable Injury Frequency) — decreasing trend
- PSER (Process Safety Event Rate) — decreasing trend
- Total maintenance cost — decreasing trend

## 5. Work management (p.6)

- Framed as "decisive factor for additional value."
- White collars: all roles evaluated, redefined, and re-placed; 5–10 FTE change-management promoters to be recruited.
- Blue collars & subcontractors: reduce waiting time, increase effective (hands-on-tool) time; shift work from subcontractors to own resources where viable ("make vs. buy"); move from hourly rates to fixed-price jobs.

## 6. Organisation (p.7)

- Goal: improve transparency and manageability.
- Organise by maintenance function, not technical discipline.
- Lean organisation: fewer organisational levels, flexible/skilled employees.
- Cost-efficient: concentrate on value-adding work (tied to KPIs), right-size headcount.

## 11. OPP plans and needs (p.13–15)

- OPP (Neste) wants to remain the most preferred partner to its clients; needs to improve safety, sustainability, productivity, adaptiveness.
- Needs to increase resource efficiency and strengthen technical capabilities — requiring remarkable changes to ways of working, practices, roles, responsibilities, and local agreements.
- Explicit acknowledgment of **strong change resistance and external attention** expected; solid top-management support and uniform communication called "essential."
- Several parallel scenarios/studies were ongoing; final plan dependent on their outcome (i.e., this was one option among several under real evaluation, not a done deal).
- Organisational diagnosis: low organisational efficiency blamed on lack of leadership/structure, overlapping/unclear roles, insufficient cost/effectiveness focus, and over-indexing on technical vs. operational expertise.
- Four strengthening priorities named: (1) leadership across short/mid/long time horizons in production lines and functions, (2) optimization of refining/manufacturing aligned with operations and maintenance, (3) energy efficiency and carbon reduction, (4) ownership of Capex projects with ability to steer toward efficiency and fit-for-purpose.

## 12. Final concept — network structure (p.17)

Concentric-ring value network model:
- Center: **End customer (One Refinery / Neste)**
- Ring 1 ("basic manageable blocks", 150 FTE): Naantali, Workshop, Warehouse, Building maintenance
- Ring 2 ("Integrator: AMOR", 300 FTE total): AMOR sits as the deep-trust integrator responsible for delivery of all services in the network
- Ring 3 (125 FTE): Main Service Providers — fewer, deeper, long-term relationships; explicit **reduction in number of service providers**
- Ring 4 (25 FTE): Other Service Providers — occasional/as-needed suppliers

## 17. Notes on savings potential (p.24) — explicit caveats in the original deck

The authors themselves flagged that the savings numbers excluded: salary/price inflation, taxes and levies, final contract content, the 1–2 year initial period before fixed pricing, turnaround (TA) effects, and — critically — **"due diligence on the figures (due diligence not performed)."** This is a first-party admission that the €43m / cost-curve numbers were indicative, not audited.

## 18. Business model description (p.26)

1. Purpose: run mutually profitable business with OPP at One Refinery; help OPP improve operations and reach business targets.
2. Relationship principle: mutual trust and respect; constant endeavour for better results for both parties.
3. Staged model: Years 1–2 transaction fee (markup/cost-plus); from Year 3 subscription fee (monthly fixed price + annual budget for shuts); from Year 3 additional external sales while prioritizing One Refinery.
4. Ownership committed for first five years; next steps to be negotiated thereafter.

## 19. Delivery model (p.27)

Cascade: **OPP strategy** (targets/needs) → **KPIs & targets: 3-year plan** (OPP strategy converted to AMOR targets) → **Annual plan** (AMOR's action plan to realize targets). Annual plan covers 15 content areas: costs; organisation/resources; maintenance operations; reliability maintenance; shutdowns/turnarounds; process & people safety; CMMS/systems; quality/information; supply chain management; stock management; lifecycle management/capex needs; statutory requirements; development projects; KPIs & reporting to OP; meeting structure.

## 22. Management buy-out structure (p.30, 32)

- New limited company **AMOR Oy** established by MMA management (Hukkinen, Ala-Nikkola, Raassina, Kekkonen).
- Business transfer from Neste to AMOR of all current MMA resources except ongoing outsourcing projects, for **a nominal price of €1**.
- ~350 FTE in scope (current MMA own resources).
- Spares/stock excluded from transfer, remain Neste-owned.
- Initial contract term: 5 years, auto-renewing annually unless terminated; termination notice 12 months, earliest exercisable after year 4. On termination, the maintenance function transfers back to Neste as-is.
- Owners remain as salaried AMOR employees, forming the core of the new management team.

## 23. Build-up of company functions (p.33)

Three non-exclusive options for support functions (payroll, IT, HR, HSE, procurement): (1) purchase from Neste, (2) transfer Neste personnel into AMOR, (3) purchase from the open market. All three to be applied as feasible.

## 24. Working groups needed (p.34)

Steering team; AMOR team (company establishment/bylaws); Contract team; HR team; Finances team; Maintenance team; Contractor team; IT/systems team; HSE team; Union negotiations; Fixed-price team (2021 due diligence and fixed-price/scope calculation).

## 25. Cost to OPP (p.35)

Estimated third-party/consultant costs: company bylaws (€20k), taxation advice (€10k), business transfer agreement (€20k), HR consultant (€50k) in 2020; fixed-price calculation consultant (€50k) in 2021.

## 26. Financial principles, Years 1–2 (p.37–38)

- Maintenance opex fully managed by AMOR, including other outsourced maintenance functions (named suppliers "Hampton, Stanley, Preston" plus building maintenance).
- AMOR's own cost: markup factor **1.25**, applied to all own cost (salaries, benefits, personnel costs, suppliers, subcontractors, materials, safety expenses) — except anything Neste supplies to AMOR free of charge.
- Administrative cost: markup factor **1.25** as well, applied because Neste provides certain systems/functions (CMMS, IT, HR, procurement, payroll) in the early stage with no separate invoicing.
- Spare parts and materials cost: **no markup** by AMOR.
- Annual cost budget/target set jointly: AMOR proposes, Neste approves; AMOR gets adjustment time before cost changes take effect; cash flow/payment terms at no additional cost to AMOR.

## 27. Financial principles, Years 3–5 (p.39)

- Maintenance opex budget fully managed by AMOR (incl. named outsourced functions).
- **Fixed price** for the maintenance baseload, covering AMOR's own personnel and daily subcontracting load.
- Annual opex budget still jointly agreed (AMOR proposes, Neste approves), with adjustment time before changes take effect.

## Extraction notes for downstream analysis

- The deck is fundamentally a **management buy-out (MBO) and outsourcing proposal**, not a performance-contracting product concept: its center of gravity is organisational transfer (350 FTE, €1 business transfer, 5-year lock-in) and cost-plus-to-fixed-price commercial progression — not KPI-linked fees, gainshare, or attribution mechanics.
- KPIs in the deck (operational availability, MTBR, TRIF, PSER, budget compliance, Solomon quartile) are used as **monitoring/success criteria for a monthly-fee relationship**, not as **payment triggers**. There is no gainshare/painshare formula, no attribution model separating maintenance-caused vs. operations-caused losses, and no explicit statement that AMOR's fee would move with plant-level performance beyond the fixed-price/hourly-rate mechanics. This is the most important structural difference from the proposed Bilfinger Asset Performance 2030 concept — see AMOR_ANALYSIS.md.
- The commercial logic of savings is almost entirely **input-cost reduction** (labour mix, hands-on-tool time, contractor consolidation, lumpsum vs. hourly) rather than **output/outcome value creation** (yield, throughput, energy efficiency, unplanned-downtime cost). This matters directly for Bilfinger, whose 2030 EBITA ambition (see BILFINGER_FACTS.md) needs a richer margin lever than cost-plus markup.
