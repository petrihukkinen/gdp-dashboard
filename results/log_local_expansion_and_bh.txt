=== Part 1: H0 x r and the DU-scaled observables (H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc) ===
  Moon      : H0*r = 8.720e-10 m/s = 2.752 cm/yr
  Earth-Sun : H0*r = 3.394e-07 m/s = 1070.969 cm/yr
  Observed LLR lunar recession (Williams & Boggs 2016, tidal): 3.83 cm/yr [from memory of paper; flagged].
  DU-scaled observables (rates in units of H0):
    coordinate round-trip time 2r/c0 : 3/2 H
    round-trip in atomic ticks       : 1 H   <- LLR range in light-seconds drifts at +H0*r
    orbital period in atomic ticks   : 1 H
    ratio P_orb/(round-trip)         : 0 H  <- pure dimensionless ratio: no signal
    Doppler: d ln r/dT / c0 -> v_r = H0*r in frequency ratio terms: present, 2.75 cm/yr for the Moon
  => Under these scalings a DU expansion of the Moon's orbit IS observable in LLR range (light-seconds) and in
     the orbital period measured in atomic seconds; it is invisible only in the ratio of the two. The claim that
     'expansion is unobservable locally' therefore depends on which observable is used; LLR is not one of them.
  Kepler check: tidal da/a -> dP/P = 1.5 da/a ; DU expansion da/a = H, dP/P(ticks) = H  (not 1.5 H) ->
     the ratio dP/P : da/a is 1.5 for tides but 1.0 for DU expansion: a second discriminating observable.

=== Part 2: injection–recovery toy (synthetic, 30 yr, monthly normal points) ===
  recovered total secular rate = 3.8302 ± 0.0012 cm/yr (injected 3.83)
  A range-only fit recovers the SUM; a separate DU term is a column identical to t -> design matrix singular:

  If ALL 3.83 cm/yr is tidal: tidal LOD increase = 2.09 ms/century (literature tidal value ≈ 2.3–2.4 ms/cy [flagged, from memory]).
  If only 1.08 cm/yr is tidal (DU takes 2.75): tidal LOD increase = 0.59 ms/century.
  The eclipse-record LOD trend (+1.7–1.8 ms/cy total, from memory; flagged) is explained by tidal 2.3–2.4 minus a
  non-tidal (GIA/core) −0.5–0.6 ms/cy. Under the DU split the non-tidal term would have to be ≈ +1.1 ms/cy of the

  variability of tidal dissipation over ~10^8 yr (not needed here: the LOD comparison is over 2700 yr).
