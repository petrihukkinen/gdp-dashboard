# Customer Duplication Test — Cycle 11

Broader orchestration only creates *system* economics if it removes or reduces duplicate
coordination somewhere. **Do not assume customer headcount reduction** — this must be
tested, not assumed in either direction.

## The two scenarios

**Additive overhead:** the provider adds a Contractor Integration Lead and the full AMOR
governance layer, but the customer retains its full existing coordination organization
unchanged. Result: pure additive cost to the system, with no netting — this directly
confirms the sharpest objection in `18_INTERVIEW_QA/CUSTOMER_CFO_VALIDATION.md` (paying for
something they already do internally). If this is what actually happens, the Accountability
Economics Thesis's system-level case does not hold, even if the provider's own P&L still
looks fine in isolation.

**Potential system economics:** the provider assumes clearly defined orchestration work
(contractor selection input, day-to-day scheduling coordination, cross-contractor
accountability), and the customer's own coordination staff either:
- **Transfer** — move to the provider (AMOR 2020's actual precedent: ~350 FTE transfer,
  `EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` A2, Level A);
- **Reduce** — the customer right-sizes its own coordination function because less is
  needed;
- **Redeploy** — the customer's staff shift to higher-value work (asset strategy, Capex
  planning) rather than day-to-day coordination.

## What stays with the customer regardless — never assumed transferable
Per `03_STRATEGIC_THESIS/ACCOUNTABILITY_BOUNDARY.md`: asset engineering/Capex planning
staff, statutory compliance staff, production/operations staff. These are non-delegable by
design, not by oversight — their retention is not evidence against duplication removal, it
is the boundary working correctly.

## What is realistically transferable, reducible, or redeployable
Contractor scheduling/dispatch coordinators, work-order dispatch/tracking staff, contractor
invoice reconciliation staff — *if* the provider's own tracking genuinely duplicates and
supersedes what these roles currently do.

## Verdict
This is a **spectrum, not a binary**, and it is untested. The Accountability Economics
Thesis's system-level case is strongest if transfer/redeploy actually happens (real
duplication removed, real value created) and weakest if the customer simply retains
everything and the provider's orchestration cost is pure addition. This is a genuinely
make-or-break variable for Mechanism 1's real economics — not a detail to assume away. It is
tested directly in `18_INTERVIEW_QA/CUSTOMER_ASSET_DIRECTOR_VALIDATION.md` Q1 ("what would
you genuinely be glad to hand off") but has no answer yet.
