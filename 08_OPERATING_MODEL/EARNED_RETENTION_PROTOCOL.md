# Earned Retention Protocol — Cycle 10

## The mandatory distinction

**Earned retention** results from superior performance, transparent governance, accumulated
asset knowledge, faster decision-making, lower coordination burden, and trusted delivery —
the customer renews because staying is genuinely better than leaving.

**Structural lock-in** results from proprietary data barriers, inaccessible systems,
undocumented knowledge, excessive transition complexity, restrictive contract terms, or
deliberate dependency — the customer renews because leaving is too costly or risky,
regardless of performance.

**AMOR 2030 must pursue earned retention only.** Structural lock-in is not a growth strategy
this project endorses — it is a customer-trust risk that directly undermines the
Accountability Economics Thesis, since the thesis depends on customers believing
consolidation and duration are genuinely in their interest, not a trap.

## Required customer protections

1. **Customer data ownership.** Data generated or collected under the contract belongs to
   the customer. The provider holds a license to use it for delivery, not ownership of it.
2. **Data portability.** A defined export format and schedule, agreed at contract start —
   not negotiated under duress at termination.
3. **Open documentation.** Processes, playbooks, and decision logic documented in a
   customer-accessible, non-proprietary form, not locked inside provider-only systems.
4. **Transition assistance.** A contractually committed support period and resource
   allocation if the customer chooses to exit — priced or included, defined at signing.
5. **Transparent KPI history.** The customer has full, continuous access to its own
   performance data throughout the relationship, not gated behind provider-controlled
   reporting.
6. **Agreed exit plan.** Defined at contract start, not improvised at termination.

## Do these protections weaken provider economics?

Tested against the mechanisms in `FIVE_ECONOMIC_MECHANISMS.md`:

- **Data ownership/portability:** modest cost if built in from the start (retrofitting
  portability later would be expensive; designing for it from day one is not). **Net
  positive** for provider economics — it directly removes the customer's biggest objection
  to Mechanisms 1 and 2 (scope consolidation, longer duration) without which those
  mechanisms may never activate at all.
- **Open documentation:** a modest ongoing discipline cost, but also simply good operating
  practice — feeds `SYSTEMATIC_CAPABILITY_MAP.md`'s "standardized transition methodology" as
  a genuine capability, not a pure cost.
- **Transition assistance commitment:** a real cost, but only if actually triggered — a
  tail cost, not a recurring one, and can be priced into exit terms (a defined transition
  fee) rather than absorbed for free.

**Verdict: these protections are net positive, not a tax on the model.** They are cheap
insurance against the larger commercial risk of never winning the broader, longer
relationship at all because the customer correctly suspects lock-in is the real intent.
Withholding them to protect short-term switching-cost economics would be self-defeating —
see the tension already flagged in the Cycle 9 reframe response between "switching costs"
as a provider benefit and "lock-in fear" as a customer blocker.
